# Getting started

## What it takes

- An email address you can verify (sign-up won't finish without clicking the confirmation link).
- A business name and your name.
- A template choice — pick the closest match to your business; every setting it implies can be
  changed later.
- Nothing to install to get going: the admin app runs in a browser. Installing the
  [Windows or Android app](./apps.md) is optional and can be done anytime afterwards.

## Sign-up steps

1. Go to the sign-up page and fill in: business name, your name, email, and a password
   (8 characters minimum).
2. Pick a template — see [Business templates](./business-templates/overview.md) for what each one
   turns on by default.
3. Check your email and click the verification link. Nothing is created until this step — this
   stops the sign-up form from being used to spam other people's inboxes.
4. Once verified, your business, its first branch, and your own admin login are created
   automatically, and you're on a **free 14-day trial** with everything switched on so you can try
   the real thing before choosing a plan.

## After you're in

A sensible order for a new business:

1. **Check your branch and currency** under Settings → Business.
2. **Add your products** — either by hand or, if your template came with sample products, start
   from those and edit them. See [Products & inventory](./modules/products-and-inventory.md).
3. **Add staff accounts** for anyone else who'll use the till or back office. See
   [Users & roles](./modules/users-and-roles.md).
4. **Make your first sale** from the POS screen — this is the fastest way to confirm stock,
   pricing, and receipts all look right.
5. **Turn on anything else you need** — an online store, pickup orders, cameras, SMS notifications
   — from Settings or [Billing → Add-ons](./billing-and-plans.md#add-ons), whether or not it came
   on by default with your template.

## If you get stuck

Every module page under [Modules](./modules/overview.md) describes what that part of the app
actually does today, including anything it *doesn't* do yet — so you can tell quickly whether
something is a setting you haven't found, or a feature that genuinely isn't built yet.
